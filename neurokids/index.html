Ventricular segmentation for paediatric hydrocephalus · head CT · runs in your browser · Research and export tools ↓

Load a head CT (DICOM folder, zip or NIfTI). NeuroKids finds the axial head series, segments the ventricular system slice by slice with the fast-radiology/hydrocephalus pipeline, and reports ventricular volume. The DICOM engine and in-browser inference come from RADAR in the browser.

We were inspired to build these education and research tools by NeuroKids, a non-profit for children with hydrocephalus and spina bifida, and by Dr Giselle Coelho and Dr Benjamin Warf. NeuroKids (this site) is an independent research project and is not run by or affiliated with NeuroKids.org.

Research use only

Not a medical device. Not validated or approved for clinical use. Do not use for diagnosis, shunt decisions or any other clinical decision.

Load a scan to enable the report buttons. Reports use the slice shown in the viewer and hold measurements and scan geometry only, no names or dates.

How it works
  1. Load. DICOM headers are grouped into series; the axial head series is decoded (Hounsfield units) and reoriented to standard axial display.
  2. AI U-Net. Each slice is windowed to −100…100 HU, histogram-equalised (bit-exact with OpenCV) and segmented at 512 × 512 by a 2D U-Net exported to ONNX and run with ONNX Runtime Web (WebGPU or multi-threaded WebAssembly). The published architecture is a ResNet-34 U-Net with self-attention trained with generalised Dice loss. A voxel is kept as ventricle where P > 0.5, then the paper's slice-to-slice consistency filter is applied.
  3. CSF baseline (no AI). Brain is found per slice as the largest soft-tissue region inside bone; voxels at −5…16 HU more than 3 mm inside the brain edge are grouped in 3D and the central components are kept. Expect it to include some cisternal CSF and to miss slit-like ventricles.
  4. Measure. Ventricular volume = voxel count × voxel volume. The agreement between the two masks is shown as a Dice coefficient.

Bring your own weights. No trained weights for the published model are public. Train on your labelled scans with tools/train.py, export with tools/export_onnx.py, then use Load .onnx or add the file to lib/models.js. See the README.
